Rohan Nath

"The study of evolution is the study of ourselves, our origins, and the very nature of life itself."
— Richard Dawkins

I am Rohan Nath, a PhD student at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Berhampur, currently pursuing an integrated MS-PhD in the Comparative and Evolutionary Genomics Laboratory under the supervision of Dr. Arunkumar Krishnan. My research focuses on the evolution, pharmacology, and pharmacogenomics of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), with the broader goal of advancing personalized approaches to drug design.

Research

01
Comparative & Evolutionary Genomics
Comparing genomic features across organisms to understand evolutionary relationships, conserved genes, and regulatory elements that illuminate how life diversifies over time.
Genomics · Evolution
02
Pharmacogenomics
Studying how genetic variation shapes individual drug response, with the aim of optimising therapeutic efficacy and minimising adverse effects through personalized medicine.
Drug Response · Precision Medicine
03
Structural Biology
Investigating three-dimensional molecular structures to understand biological function and design targeted therapies — particularly for G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs).
GPCRs · Drug Design
04
Phylogenomics
Using genome-scale data to reconstruct evolutionary relationships among species, resolving complex phylogenetic histories and the genetic basis of adaptation.
Phylogeny · Adaptation
